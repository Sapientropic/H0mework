# Refusal Does Not Delete the Bearer

> **Editorial draft - three-page flagship.** Refusal neither executes its own demand nor expels the
> refusing bearer from standing.

## Page 1 of 3 - Between automatic victory and institutional exile

Appeal theory often oscillates between two bad models. In the first, a complaint is real only if it
wins. In the second, rejection closes the file and the institution treats the claimant, reason, and
unresolved burden as if they had disappeared.

Process-generated recourse takes a harder position:

\[
\boxed{
\text{rejection does not write the world}
\quad\land\quad
\text{rejection does not delete the bearer}.
}
\]

A lawful refusal must preserve:

- the source-owned proposal or claim;
- the exact typed reason for rejection;
- the standing lineage of the affected bearer;
- the residual created or left open by rejection;
- an independent consumer of that residual;
- an actionable route to a later occurrence.

The rejected proposal does not thereby become law. The institution does not thereby gain the right
to erase the claimant. Recourse is the generated future between those two errors.
